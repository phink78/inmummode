Mum Mode

The last three hours of the day, written down.

A free seven-night guide. Four minutes an evening. The first two nights you change nothing at all — you only watch, because almost everyone fixes the wrong hour.

And it's written to you, not to the mother of someone. Everyone else in your week is already talking to the role.

What's in it

  • Two nights of recording the evening exactly as it happens, so you can find the real pinch point instead of the loudest one.
  • One decision moved to the morning, where it costs you almost nothing.
  • One thing removed entirely — subtraction, not substitution.
  • Five sentences for the handover, when there's another adult and it keeps turning into a negotiation.
  • A twenty-minute reset for the evenings that have already gone.
  • A two-line nightly log. It takes longer to read this sentence than to fill it in.

One email with the guide. A short note each week after that, and an unsubscribe link in every one. Nothing else, and your address goes nowhere else.

Where this comes from

Timing, sequence, and the number of decisions you carry into the worst hour of the day. All of it drawn from published work on routine and decision load rather than from anyone's particular Tuesday — which is why it's a structure to test in your house, not a story about someone else's.

It is not therapy, and it is not medical, feeding or sleep advice. Nothing here tells you when your child should sleep or eat.
